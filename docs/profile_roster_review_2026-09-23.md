## Scope and how to read the numbers

This review joins all **423 offensive players** in the captured league pool to
their canonical profiles, including **all 15 offensive players on your roster**.
It screens the entire available pool and reviews the leading candidates against
NFL season histories, weekly scoring distributions, participation history,
current usage, team changes, draft information, accepted college links, historical
injury records, current injury evidence and bye weeks. All 15 roster players have
accepted college links; some other players have missing or partial college data.
The 70 K/DST entries are outside these offensive models, including your four.

- Ownership was refreshed at **4:22 p.m. EDT, September 23**. All recommended
  additions were unrostered. The snapshot does not distinguish an immediate add
  from a player subject to waivers. Mayfield was dropped earlier that day.
- NFL usage ends at **Week 2**. The league uses its own full-PPR scoring and bonuses,
  with one QB, two RBs, three WRs, one TE and one RB/WR/TE flex.
- The final review pins completed **`nextgen_system_20260923_r2`**, corrected gold
  **`canonical_nextgen_20260923_r1`**, and the matching NextGen profile, college and
  outlook products. All 22,233 forecast values match the preceding r1 release;
  r2 adds extrapolation diagnostics. None of the captured players' season-points
  forecasts is flagged outside the training target range. That is not a guarantee
  of accuracy or current-role suitability.
- **Usage / outlook** means two separate estimates of total points in **Weeks 3–6**,
  with byes included. **Ridge / boost** means two separate **preseason full-season**
  forecasts. These are not intervals, rest-of-season forecasts or interchangeable
  horizons. They are not averaged into an invented overall score.
- The research models remain unpromoted. The historical baseline is the approved
  default. The rank order below is my decision judgment using the evidence, not a
  validated optimal waiver algorithm or proof of an advantage over the market.
- No external rankings or projections enter the calculations. External reporting
  supplies injury context only. “NextGen” means this project's own system.

Historical points per observed week include weeks with recorded statistics or
positive offensive snaps, including partial appearances. They are not points per
healthy start. Medians and averages excluding the best two weeks describe the
past; they do not establish a future floor. Missing histories remain unknown.
Profiles contextualize the existing forecasts; this review does not refit them.

## 1. Replacing Travis Hunter

Hunter's profile makes the distinction particularly clear. His 2024 Colorado
season recorded **96 catches, 1,258 yards and 15 receiving TDs**. In seven observed
NFL weeks in 2025 he averaged **9.1 points**, with 45 targets. Those are reasons
to respect the talent. His current offensive role is nevertheless just **one
target across two weeks**, with **7% and 10%** of offensive snaps. His Weeks 3–6
usage/outlook estimates are **8.8 / 7.0**. The replacement recommendation is based
on that role, not a claim that his college profile was weak.

### Bench allocation and secondary additions

**I prefer Johnson → Tate as the third move**, after Mitchell and Mayfield.
You start three WRs plus a flex, Moore has a current injury concern, and Rice's
bye arrives in Week 5. McBride does not need bye coverage until Week 14. A second
receiver has more paths into this roster's lineup than a backup TE. This is a
roster-construction judgment, not a claim that Tate will necessarily score more
raw points than Johnson. It also preserves Doubs and both K/DST pairs. If Mitchell
is unavailable and Tate is the Hunter replacement, Tucker becomes my preferred
additional receiver for Johnson's spot.

**Schultz → Johnson is an alternative if you want immediate TE/flex production.**
Schultz's 22 current targets and **44.3 / 43.5** usage/outlook estimates beat
Johnson's 11 and **30.0 / 29.7**. The profile comparison is much closer: 2025 means
10.5 versus 10.6; medians 11.0 versus 10.6; averages without the best two weeks
9.2 versus 9.6. Both preseason models favor Johnson. Nico Collins has a Week 1
profile row but no Week 2 row, and is currently questionable; Schultz's 14-target
Week 2 is thus a workload-concentration caution. We do not have a verified
duration for that opportunity. Otton and Henry have useful profiles but weaker
current demand. Do not carry McBride, Johnson and Schultz together for this plan.

**Marks → Black is a reasonable optional improvement for standalone PPR use.**
Marks has seven current targets, compared with Black's one, and preseason
ridge/boost forecasts of **115.6 / 119.6** versus **95.2 / 76.1**. His **34.3 / 34.5**
four-week estimates also exceed Black's **26.4 / 27.1**. The profile supports a
receiving skill history: Marks recorded 47 catches in his final USC season and
83 in his 2021 Mississippi State season; Black recorded four in each Indiana
season. College systems differ, so these counts are context, not a translation
formula. Marks' 2025 NFL median was only 7.3 and only 5/16 weeks reached ten points.
His present six-target Week 2 also coincided with Houston's changed target mix.
Keep Black instead if CMC contingency protection matters more than standalone
depth. No modeled CMC injury probability or guaranteed successor workload is
available to price that insurance precisely.

**Week 8 remains an RB planning issue either way.** McCaffrey, Etienne, Black
**and Marks** share that bye. Pollard and Dowdle both have Week 9 byes, so those two
could cover Week 8 if healthy. A Marks acquisition cannot be justified as solving
the overlap. Revisit a healthy, non-Week-8 RB closer to the bye if those options
remain doubtful; the present free-agent alternatives do not justify forcing a
weaker acquisition now. Emanuel Wilson is already rostered by Pause.

## Concrete priority and what changed

1. **Hunter → Mitchell.** Fallback order for immediate depth: Tucker, Tate, Wicks,
   Bateman; move Tate ahead of Tucker if the slot is purely developmental.
2. **Dart → Mayfield.** Stroud first instead if near-term QB starts are the goal;
   otherwise Stroud is the first fallback, then Young, Love and Ward for this roster.
3. **Johnson → Tate** for an additional WR stash; Tucker if Tate is already added
   or unavailable. This is my preferred discretionary roster improvement.
4. **Black → Marks**, optionally, for more standalone receiving opportunity.
   Preserve Black if prioritizing CMC insurance.
5. **Johnson → Schultz** is an alternative to step 3, not an additional move
   using the same drop. It serves near-term TE/flex usage rather than WR depth.

The profile audit strengthens Mayfield, tempers Mitchell's certainty, moves
Tucker ahead of Tate for immediate use, protects Rice/Sutton/Doubs from a
two-week overreaction, and weakens the idea that Schultz is a clear permanent
upgrade over Johnson. It also identifies the Week 8 RB overlap and the better
use of a second-TE bench spot. None of the small four-week differences is a
guaranteed lineup gain: replacing a bench player does not add the entire
incoming-minus-outgoing total to your starting lineup, and competing additions
cannot all occupy the same flex slot.
